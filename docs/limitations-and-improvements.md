## Unresolved evidence gaps

**Representativeness.** Exhibit B is selected external estimated insured loss, with no sampling frame or exposure denominator. Mechanism groups have unequal and sometimes small counts. The 28-observation candidate group still precedes policy mapping and may not resemble the target book.

**Confounding and model structure.** Oversight can proxy governance, context and application complexity. The binary flag does not show whether review was effective. Additive effects suppress interactions, and random-fold validation cannot establish temporal or customer-population transportability.

**Frequency and contract adjustment.** No AI policy-year claim frequency, deductible/limit structure, defence-development pattern or recoveries are given. An external event estimate cannot be substituted for insured payout under proposed terms.

**Capital and accumulation.** The case omits available capital, reserves, risk appetite, treaty wording and dependency-level exposures. Neither accounting profit nor nominal limit totals demonstrate solvency capacity.

**Historical external extracts.** The original deck reports 128 AIID/CSET severe-harm observations and 477 DAIL lawsuits. The underlying extraction and filtering rules were not supplied. These figures are not reproduced or retained as quantitative decision evidence. A reported 46.7% industry statistic also fails the displayed 59/128 calculation; that arithmetic is about 46.1%, and categories must be checked for overlap before combining counts.

**Demand transferability.** The Geneva Association survey covers six markets excluding Canada. It supports a demand hypothesis, not a Canadian SME conversion rate. Canada's 12.2% historical AI usage and 14.5% expected future usage come from different survey periods and can overlap; they must not be added.

## Additional data that would change the decision

1. Deduplicated customer-level AI inventory, use cases, roles, permissions and vendor dependencies.
2. Willingness-to-pay and quote conversion for a clearly specified incremental coverage grant.
3. Policy-year exposure and claims/notifications with defence and indemnity development.
4. Verified oversight quality, change logs, incidents and near misses.
5. Capital/risk budget, reserve uncertainty and confirmed common-event reinsurance response.

## Presentation revision

The main narrative is reduced to 10 slides; four appendix slides retain definitions, financial detail, model interpretation and source boundaries. Numerical evidence uses editable charts and tables. The cover keeps the original competition identity and all four names. Supporting caveats that affect the recommendation remain visible; detailed calculations and sources move to notes and this repository.

The package distinguishes original team work from subsequent portfolio revisions. It does not claim a competition placement, implementation outcome, revenue impact, individual analytical ownership, or verified insurance pricing that the supplied materials do not establish.
