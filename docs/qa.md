# Decision Q&A

**Why B rather than A?** Existing customer information and distribution may make a limited endorsement cheaper to implement. This advantage must be tested. A narrow standalone product can also control scope and may be preferable if customers need a distinct promise.

**Why not choose C?** C is appropriate when demand, pricing, wording or capacity gates are unmet. It should have concrete capability-building outputs and a review date, rather than an indefinite delay.

**What does the customer buy that it does not already have?** A specified affirmative grant, exclusion buyback, sublimit or defence arrangement. The coverage comparison must precede an additional charge.

**Does 84% combined ratio prove capacity?** It demonstrates historical underwriting profit. Available capital, reserves and reinsurance remain separate.

**Why exclude autonomous applications initially?** Autonomous failures concentrate estimated severity in this sample, including nonphysical failures. The initial appetite is conservative because consequences and common dependencies remain difficult to calibrate, not because autonomy is universally uninsurable.

**Does supervision make every risk acceptable?** No. Four autonomous failures also have oversight marked Yes. Purpose, permissions, consequences, control quality and contract fit all matter.

**Does oversight cause a 35% reduction?** No causal estimate is established. The additive log model gives a conditional geometric-mean association, and severity alone does not determine price.

**Why is the industry share unstable?** Industry and mechanism overlap. A sequential decomposition allocates their shared information to whichever enters first.

**Can the incident data price frequency?** No. The data lack exposure and non-incident observations. Event counts are sample composition.

**What would make the pilot uneconomic?** Low customer willingness to pay, high fixed/verification costs, higher covered frequency or severity, defence development, reinsurance cost, or concentration beyond supported capacity.

**What does the first renewal prove?** It provides evidence about disclosure, controls, retention and early claim development. One year without claims does not validate long-tail profitability.

**Can TSI exit?** It can restrict future binding and renewal subject to contract and law. Existing obligations and open claims remain.

**What should the board approve?** An appetite, risk budget, launch gates and escalation rules. Management must translate those into legally reviewed terms, supported capacity and calibrated pricing before binding.
